The short version

  • Taiwanese Mandarin is Mandarin. It is not the same language as Taiwanese (Taigi). The tones and grammar are basically the same, with some Taiwan-specific words and pronunciations.
  • Taiwan uses Zhuyin, not Pinyin, to show how words sound, and Traditional characters, not Simplified.
  • Start with sounds. Zhuyin first, about 10 minutes a day, then books with Zhuyin printed beside the characters.

First, Mandarin or Taiwanese?

This trips up a lot of families, so it’s worth sorting out before you buy a single book. In Taiwan, “Mandarin” is 國語 (guóyǔ) or 華語 (huáyǔ). It’s the main language of school and TV, and of a lot of daily life. “Taiwanese” usually means 台語 (Taigi, or Taiwanese Hokkien), a different language that many older people speak at home.

Plenty of grandparents switch between the two in one sentence. If you’re not sure which one your kids hear, ask: “Is that 國語 or 台語?” This page is about Taiwanese Mandarin.

In Taiwan, kids learn Zhuyin at the start of first grade, before they read many characters. Once they know the symbols, a picture book with Zhuyin beside every character is something they can start sounding out on their own. For heritage kids in the US, that’s the appeal: they can follow along in the books relatives send before they know the characters.

Words that differ (and why it’s not a big deal)

The grammar and the four tones are basically the same. What changes is some everyday vocabulary and a few pronunciations. A handful your kids may notice:

MeaningCommon in TaiwanCommon in mainland China
Bicycle腳踏車自行车
Pineapple鳳梨菠萝
Taxi計程車出租车
Subway捷運地铁

In Taiwan you’ll also tend to hear less of the “-r” sound at the end of words (哪裡 instead of 哪儿). If your kids pick up both versions from different relatives, that’s fine. Mandarin speakers from Taiwan and mainland China generally understand each other just fine.

How to start at home

  1. Weeks 1–2: the first symbols by ear. ㄅ ㄆ ㄇ ㄈ plus ㄚ spell words your kids already say, like 爸 (ㄅㄚˋ, dad) and 媽 (ㄇㄚ, mom). About 10 minutes a day.
  2. Weeks 3–6: the rest of the 37. Keep going down the Bopomofo chart, one or two symbols at a time, and play with the four tones out loud.
  3. After that: real books. Pick a picture book with Zhuyin beside the characters. Your child reads the Zhuyin, you point to the character. The characters come later, a few at a time.

If your weekend Chinese school uses Pinyin

A lot of Chinese schools in the US teach Pinyin and Simplified characters, and some offer a Traditional or Zhuyin class. Ask which track the class uses before you sign up. If the school uses Pinyin, you can still do Zhuyin at home. Just tell your kids that the two are different ways of spelling the same sounds. Frequently asked questions

Is Taiwanese Mandarin the same as Taiwanese?

No. Taiwanese Mandarin is the Mandarin Chinese spoken in Taiwan, called 國語 or 華語 there. Taiwanese (台語, also called Taigi or Taiwanese Hokkien) is a separate language. Many grandparents speak both, so it helps to know which one your family means.

Do kids in Taiwan learn Zhuyin or Pinyin?

Zhuyin. Elementary schools in Taiwan teach Zhuyin (Bopomofo) at the start of first grade, before kids read many characters. Pinyin is the standard in mainland China.

Should my child learn Traditional or Simplified characters?

If your family's Mandarin comes from Taiwan, Traditional characters match the books, signs and messages your child will see there. Zhuyin is printed next to Traditional characters in many Taiwanese children's books, so a child who knows Zhuyin can start sounding those books out.

My child goes to a weekend Chinese school that teaches Pinyin. Can we still do Zhuyin at home?

Yes, but tell your child that the two systems spell the same sounds in different ways. Some families keep Pinyin for school and use Zhuyin for books and video calls with family in Taiwan. If it causes mix-ups, pause one for a few weeks.
