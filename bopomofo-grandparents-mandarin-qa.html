4 questions from bilingual families

Why Bopomofo, if the grandparents mostly speak to my child?

Bopomofo, also called Zhuyin, is the phonetic system children in Taiwan learn before reading many characters, so it is often what Taiwanese grandparents learned themselves. Children's books from Taiwan print the symbols next to the text, which lets a grandparent and child read the same page together.

I don't read Chinese. Can I still help my child practise?

Yes. Lumi Bopomofo runs on listening and tapping: your child hears a sound, picks the matching symbol and feeds it to a friend. The app has 44 interface languages, including English, and Today's Review brings back the symbols your child hasn't mastered yet, so you don't need to track them.

What age is it for, and what does it practise?

It is built for ages 4 to 7 and covers all 37 Bopomofo symbols. Magic Tracing practises stroke order with a finger, the Tone Coaster helps kids hear the four tones, and the Sound Train links initial, medial and final into a syllable. It teaches Bopomofo only, not Pinyin.

Can my child use it on their own?

Lumi Bopomofo is completely ad-free, needs no sign-up and collects no personal data; progress is stored only on the device. The listing also notes offline play. It runs on iPhone and iPad, and there is an Android version on Google Play.

Want your child to share Bopomofo with their grandparents? Lumi Bopomofo is a free download. Your child can play the first symbol for free; after that, a one-time in-app purchase unlocks all 37 symbols and every level. No subscription, no ads, no sign-up, and progress is stored only on the device.
