Bopomofo (Zhuyin) chart — all 37 symbols

Bopomofo, also called Zhuyin (注音符號), is the phonetic alphabet children in Taiwan use to learn Mandarin pronunciation. It has 37 symbols: 21 initials (consonants), 3 medials and 13 finals, plus five tone marks. The tables below list every symbol with its Pinyin equivalent, a rough English sound hint and an everyday example word you can say with your child.

How to read this chart

Every Mandarin syllable is built from up to three parts in a fixed order: an initial (the opening consonant), an optional medial (ㄧ, ㄨ or ㄩ), and a final (the vowel ending). A tone mark sits after the last symbol. For example, 花 “flower” is written ㄏㄨㄚ: ㄏ (h) + ㄨ (u) + ㄚ (a), first tone, which Pinyin writes as huā.

The English hints are approximations to help an English-speaking parent get close. Mandarin has sounds English does not, so listen to a native speaker or an official recording whenever you can. The key thing many parents miss: ㄅ ㄉ ㄍ are not voiced like English b, d, g. They are crisp, unaspirated sounds, while ㄆ ㄊ ㄎ are the same mouth positions with a strong puff of air. Hold a tissue in front of your mouth: it should flutter for ㄆ but stay still for ㄅ.

The traditional recitation order, which children in Taiwan learn as a chant, is ㄅㄆㄇㄈ ㄉㄊㄋㄌ ㄍㄎㄏ ㄐㄑㄒ ㄓㄔㄕㄖ ㄗㄘㄙ ㄚㄛㄜㄝ ㄞㄟㄠㄡ ㄢㄣㄤㄥ ㄦ ㄧㄨㄩ. Below, the symbols are grouped by job instead, which is easier for explaining how syllables are spelled.

Notice the families. ㄅㄆㄇㄈ use the lips. ㄉㄊㄋㄌ use the tongue tip behind the teeth. ㄍㄎㄏ come from the back of the mouth. ㄐㄑㄒ are made with a flat tongue and spread lips. ㄓㄔㄕㄖ curl the tongue tip back, and ㄗㄘㄙ keep it straight behind the front teeth. Teaching in these families of three or four makes the 21 initials far less overwhelming.

The seven symbols ㄓ ㄔ ㄕ ㄖ ㄗ ㄘ ㄙ can stand alone as a whole syllable. Zhuyin writes 知 “know” as just ㄓ, while Pinyin needs a vowel letter and writes zhī. That is why the Pinyin column shows “zh (zhi)”.

3 medials (介音 ㄧ ㄨ ㄩ)

ZhuyinPinyinSounds roughly likeExample word
ㄧi (yi)like ee in “see”一 yī — one
ㄨu (wu)like oo in “food”五 wǔ — five
ㄩü (yu)say “ee” while rounding your lips, like German ü魚 yú — fish

Medials sit between an initial and a final, as in ㄒㄧㄚˋ (下 xià, down) or ㄍㄨㄛˊ (國 guó, country). They can also be a syllable on their own, or start one: ㄧ is yi, ㄧㄚ is ya, ㄨ is wu, ㄨㄛ is wo, ㄩ is yu. Pinyin adds y- or w- in those cases, but Zhuyin simply writes the medial.

After ㄐ ㄑ ㄒ, the medial ㄩ is written in Pinyin as a plain u: ㄐㄩ is ju, ㄑㄩ is qu. Zhuyin always shows ㄩ, which removes a classic stumbling block for kids who already read English.

The finals come in natural groups: four simple vowels (ㄚㄛㄜㄝ), four diphthongs (ㄞㄟㄠㄡ), four nasal endings (ㄢㄣㄤㄥ) and the curled ㄦ. The pairs ㄢ/ㄤ and ㄣ/ㄥ are the ones most families mix up. Say 山 ㄕㄢ (shān) and 傷 ㄕㄤ (shāng) back to back: ㄤ and ㄥ end at the back of the mouth, like the end of “sing”.

ㄝ is almost never a syllable by itself; you mainly meet it after a medial, as in ㄧㄝ (ye) and ㄩㄝ (yue).

The same sounds ㄇㄚ mean five different things depending on the tone, which is why children should practice tones from day one rather than “adding them later”. In Zhuyin, the first tone is normally written with no mark at all, and the neutral-tone dot goes in front of the syllable in horizontal text (on top in vertical text). Tone marks ˊ ˇ ˋ follow the last symbol of the syllable.

A playful trick: draw the tone shape in the air with your hand while saying the word. Flat line for the first tone, a slope up for the second, a “check mark” for the third, and a slope down for the fourth.

Frequently asked questions

How many Bopomofo symbols are there?

There are 37 basic symbols: 21 initials, 3 medials (ㄧ ㄨ ㄩ) and 13 finals. On top of these there are four tone marks (ˉ, usually omitted, plus ˊ ˇ ˋ) and the neutral-tone dot ˙.

Is Bopomofo the same as Zhuyin?

Yes. Zhuyin fuhao (注音符號) is the official name; “Bopomofo” comes from the first four symbols ㄅㄆㄇㄈ. Both names refer to the same system used in Taiwan.

Why does ㄅ sound like “p” to me?

Because Mandarin separates sounds by breath, not by voicing. ㄅ is an unaspirated p/b sound, like the p in “spot”; ㄆ has a strong puff of air, like the p in “pot”.

Where can I hear official pronunciations?

Taiwan’s Ministry of Education publishes a Mandarin Phonetic Symbols handbook online, linked in the sources below. It is in Chinese and includes the official symbol order and rules.
