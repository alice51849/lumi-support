What is Bopomofo? 4 questions parents ask before downloading a kids app

Many parents in the US first meet the word "Bopomofo" when a grandparent, a weekend school or a Taiwanese storybook mentions it. Here is a plain answer to what it is, plus the questions parents ask before downloading Lumi Bopomofo. Everything about the app comes from its App Store listing.

4 questions before you download

What is Bopomofo, and is it the same as Zhuyin?

Yes. Bopomofo is the everyday nickname for Zhuyin, the phonetic symbols used in Taiwan to show how Mandarin is pronounced. The nickname comes from its first symbols, ㄅ ㄆ ㄇ ㄈ (b, p, m, f). Children in Taiwan learn these symbols before reading many characters, and children's books there often print them next to the text.

Does my child need to read Chinese characters before starting Bopomofo?

No. Bopomofo is about sounds, so it usually comes before characters. Lumi Bopomofo is built for ages 4 to 7 and starts by ear: kids hear a sound, pick the matching symbol and feed it to a friend, then trace each symbol in stroke order.

I don't read Chinese. Can I still help my child use the app?

Yes. The app has 44 interface languages, so you can use it in English, and the games run on listening and tapping. Today's Review brings back the symbols your child hasn't mastered yet, so you don't need to track them yourself.
