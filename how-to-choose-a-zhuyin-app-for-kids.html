First, decide who the app is for

  • A preschooler or young child who is just meeting ㄅㄆㄇ: look for listening games, stroke-order tracing and short sessions.
  • A teen or adult learning Mandarin: you will want structured lessons, typing, pinyin side by side, and real reading. Most kids' apps (ours included) are the wrong fit.
  • A family that already reads characters: you may need graded reading more than symbol drills.

Where Lumi Bopomofo falls short

  • No ratings yet in the US. The other apps above have real reviews you can read; we do not.
  • Symbols, tones and blending only. It is not a reading course: no graded stories, no character lessons and no typing practice. Once your child can blend syllables, you will need something else for reading.
  • Zhuyin only, no pinyin. If your family uses pinyin as a bridge, an app that shows both will suit you better.
  • Made for young children. Big buttons, cute companions and voice prompts. Teens and adults will find it too young.
  • No teacher accounts or class dashboard. Progress stays on the device, so a teacher cannot see a whole class.
  • The free version includes only the first symbol. The full set of 37 symbols and every level needs the one-time in-app purchase.

Who we think should pick Lumi Bopomofo

  • Parents of a preschooler, or of a child getting ready for first grade in a Taiwan-style curriculum, who want listening, tracing, tones and blending in one place.
  • Families who prefer one purchase, no ads and no sign-up. Neither edition collects personal data, and the Pro edition needs no internet connection.
  • Weekend Chinese school teachers who want worksheets to print (Pro: tracing-and-quiz sheets of learned or recently missed sounds).

Schools can buy apps in volume through Apple School Manager.

Who should pick something else

  • Adults and teens learning Mandarin: a course-style app with typing and characters is a better match.
  • Families who mainly need reading practice: an app with graded stories will do more.
  • Anyone who wants to read many parent reviews before choosing: the apps with ratings above give you that today.

A note on screen time

No app replaces a grown-up saying the sounds with a child. Short sessions and reading together matter more than which app you choose.
