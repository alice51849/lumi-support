How to choose

  1. Read the average rating together with the number of ratings: 4.8 from 30 ratings says much less than 4.6 from 30,000. A last update within the past few months suggests the app is still maintained and keeps up with current iOS versions.
  2. Look at the age rating and whether the app shows ads or links out of the app. For children, a parental gate in front of purchases and settings matters more than the number of features.
  3. Short daily sessions work better than long, rare ones for most learners, so check whether the app lets you set a small daily goal, keeps progress you can review, and still works offline.
  4. The price column shows the list price only. Many free apps sell subscriptions or in-app purchases, which the API does not report, so open the App Store page and check the “In-App Purchases” section before you rely on an app.
